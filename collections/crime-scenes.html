Crime Scenes

Robberies, hangings, frauds and the courtrooms that sorted them out.

  1. Bad Ends
    On August 30, 1850, Boston hanged a Harvard chemistry professor about here.
  2. Bad Ends
    A dentist identified Parkman from a set of false teeth he'd made in 1846.
    2 North Grove Street (outside, on the street)
  3. Bad Ends
    The plinth says she was hanged on the Common. A judge's diary points a mile south.
    Beacon Street just west of Bowdoin Street, below the State House east wing
  4. Bad Ends
    The stone people call Hester Prynne's may belong to a woman the court found 'greatly negligent' in her child's death.
    Tremont Street at School Street, next to King's Chapel (58 Tremont St)
  5. Bad Ends
    The best old source on the 'hanging tree' is a surgeon's pamphlet, and he meant hanging in effigy.
    On the Common, uphill from Frog Pond, toward Tremont and West Streets
  6. Bad Ends
    A 1911 paper put Boston's Quaker gallows out here on the Neck, using a judge's diary and a 1722 map.
    Washington Street at East Berkeley Street, South End
  7. Banned in Boston
    In the 1890s a public speech on the Common needed the mayor's permit, and Oliver Wendell Holmes said that was fine.
    Brewer Fountain Plaza, inside the Common from the Park Street station headhouses
  8. Banned in Boston
    The Censors' Last Address
  9. Big Scores
    The Jail That Became a Hotel
    215 Charles Street, at Cambridge Street, beside Charles/MGH station
  10. Big Scores
    Somebody Stole the Weathervane
    Faneuil Hall, North Street side (Faneuil Hall Square)
  11. Big Scores
    James Michael Curley went to jail in 1904 and to federal prison in 1947, the second time while he was mayor. His tablet mentions neither.
    Union Street just north of North Street (near 19 North Street)
  12. Big Scores
    In 1950 this building lost more than $1.2 million in cash in about twenty minutes. Today you can rent a space in it by the hour.
    Prince Street at Commercial Street, North End (FBI: 165 Prince Street; today listed as 600 Commercial Street)
  13. Big Scores
    In 1765 a Boston crowd took about £900 in cash from the lieutenant governor's house, by his own count, and stripped it to the floorboards.
    Garden Court at Prince Street, North End (west side of Garden Court, just before North Square)
  14. Big Scores
    Customs seized John Hancock's sloop Liberty at this wharf in 1768, and the waterfront rioted. The smuggling case against him was dropped.
    Lewis Wharf, 28 Atlantic Avenue, at the foot of Fleet Street
  15. Big Scores
    The pirate William Fly retied his own noose in 1726. Boston hung his body in chains on a harbor island as a notice to every ship coming in.
    Christopher Columbus Park path, west of the Harborwalk (near 110 Atlantic Avenue)
  16. Big Scores
    Excessive Charges
    Washington Street, west side, opposite the Old State House (just south of Court Street)
  17. Big Scores
    Captain Kidd was held on this lot in 1699 in sixteen pounds of iron, and the governor paid the sheriff weekly to keep him there.
  18. Big Scores
    Ponzi took in $9,582,000 in eight months from an office up the stairs here, by the Supreme Court's count.
  19. Big Scores
    The Boston Post won the 1921 Pulitzer Prize for exposing Ponzi, and this block is what's left of the street it printed on.
    Milk Street just east of Washington Street (Transcript Building, 322-328 Washington / 5-11 Milk; Post Building, 15-17 Milk)
  20. Big Scores
    Captain Kidd was arrested at the door of the governor's lodging in July 1699 - the house that stood here.
    327 Washington Street, west side, opposite Milk Street
  21. Big Scores
    Sarah Howe ran a bank for single women at eight percent a month in 1879, and went to prison for it, forty years before Ponzi.
  22. Big Scores
    Thirteen works were taken from the Gardner in 1990, and the empty frames still hang where they were.
    280 The Fenway (museum entrance 25 Evans Way)
