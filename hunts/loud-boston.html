Pack 09

A Boston Music History Walking Tour, Venue by Venue

Boston has always been loud in specific rooms. This walk goes to them. It starts at a stone meeting house in the South End that spent two and a half years as the Boston Tea Party, the room where the Velvet Underground felt at home and Led Zeppelin made their debut in town. It follows Storyville, George Wein's jazz club, from a hotel on Huntington Avenue to Kenmore Square and back, and stops over the cellar on Boylston Street where two clubs shared one staircase and a lot of famous people played before they were famous. In Kenmore you stand where the Rat was, and on Lansdowne Street you find out what the same address has been through since it was a taxi garage behind the Green Monster. Then it turns to the halls: the movie house Berklee bought, the concert hall a Harvard physicist helped design, a department-store family's gift to the Conservatory, and the opera house that left nothing but a street name. It ends on Mass Ave at the one club on the old Jazz Corner of Boston that's still open. Every stop has a riddle that gets you there and an answer you read off the building, and every story comes with its sources, including the ones that correct what you've heard. The walk is about two and three-quarter hours, flat and step-free, and there's a one-hour short cut through the Symphony and Mass Ave stops. Two detours go out to Aerosmith's old Allston apartment and Club Passim in Harvard Square. Any two stops are free.

Stop 01 · South End

Lou Reed called this old church the Velvet Underground's 'favorite place to play in the whole country.'

On December 12, 1968, Lou Reed called this old church the Velvet Underground's 'favorite place to play in the whole country.' From January 20, 1967, the meeting house on this corner was the Boston Tea Party, with Ray Riepen running it and the Boston band the Lost on the first bill. It had been showing underground films; the weekend dance concerts were supposed to pay for them. Within two years Led Zeppelin had made their Tea Party debut and B.B. King had played the room. Now look up at the star in the window. That star is why you'll read, on Wikipedia among other places, that this was built as a synagogue. The marker by the door says it went up in 1872 as a Unitarian meeting house named for Theodore Parker, and that the star was about bringing the world's religions together. Second story you'll hear: WBCN, the station that made Boston an album-rock town, started in the Tea Party's back room. Riepen founded the station too, but its own people put the first studio upstairs at 171 Newbury Street and call the back-room version a myth. The club moved to Lansdowne Street in July 1969. You'll see that block later.

Look now

The star in the window, then the marker by the door. Same building, two different stories about it.

Stop 02 · Back Bay

Charlie Parker was booked to play the jazz club in this hotel on the night he died.

Charlie Parker was booked to play this hotel on March 12, 1955. He died in New York that night. The room was Storyville, and George Wein had opened it here in October 1950, at twenty-five, naming it after New Orleans' old red-light district. About six weeks in he fell out with the landlord and closed. He reopened in Kenmore Square in February 1951, came back here in September 1953 under new hotel owners, and shut it for good in May 1960. In between, Louis Armstrong, Billie Holiday, Miles Davis, Ella Fitzgerald, Thelonious Monk and Lester Young played Storyville. The critic Nat Hentoff said listening there was like sitting at home with a pair of earphones. Wein didn't stay a club owner. A Storyville regular, a BU English professor, introduced him to Elaine and Louis Lorillard, and with them he started the Newport Jazz Festival in 1954, then Newport Folk in 1959. The hotel was built in 1891, and Lost New England calls it the only nineteenth-century building left on Huntington between Copley Square and Mass Ave. Hold up the 1909 photo: the building has barely changed. The rail yard just out of frame became the Prudential Center.

Look now

Hold the 1909 photo up to the hotel - the building hasn't changed much; everything around it has.

Detours

  • Aerosmith's Allston Apartment
    From Lansdowne Street walk back to Kenmore station (about 5 minutes) and take the Green Line B branch outbound toward Boston College to Harvard Avenue, about 15 minutes. Walk west along Commonwealth Avenue to number 1325, about 6 minutes. Come back the same way. Fare applies - check before you go.
  • The Folk Club That Kept Its Number
    From the Orpheum, walk to Park Street station (2 minutes) and take the Red Line toward Alewife four stops to Harvard, about 10 minutes. Palmer Street is a few minutes' walk from the station exit in Harvard Square. Come back the same way. Fare applies - check before you go.
